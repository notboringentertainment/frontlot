## Goal

Let a project produce and judge one governed shot as soon as the entities that shot depicts are approved, with three creative decisions from Ben instead of a decision per paid operation, while keeping canon, asset and spending protections. Unapproved or absent assets still cannot enter a shot; existing receipts stay valid; a finished shot cannot mark the visual bible or the film complete.

Acceptance criterion (Codex, agreed): Ben attaches the approved canon and character references to one chosen shot, reviews the shot package and its spending allowance, approves the storyboard, generates a take, and accepts or rejects that take with its provenance intact. Unused locations, unused sheets and the poster do not block the shot.

## The interaction, start to finish

One-time setup (once per project, not per shot):
- S0 Ben signs `pipeline-migration-1-6` on the board. The only new mandatory interruption this plan introduces.

Per shot, three decisions:
- **D-A Shot package + allowance** (conversation, recorded). Codex presents one package: the story beat with canon ids, the setting in words, the approved references it will pack, intended action, duration, endpoint, the production steps, a spending allowance in dollars, and a take limit. Ben gives one decision. What is recorded: the `script` and `scene_plan` checkpoints at `completed` with `human_approved: true`, the allowance and take limit on the shot record, and the conversation. These flags record an approval; they do not prove one. They are not signatures and are not described as such.
- **D-B Storyboard** (board, signed). Codex generates one frame per shot (paid, inside the allowance). Ben sees the frame on the board and signs `storyboard_batch`. Existing mandatory gate; this plan does not remove it. A redone frame needs a new batch signature (existing rule), so storyboard retries are the one place a retry still interrupts Ben.
- **D-C Footage** (board, signed if Q2 chosen). Codex generates takes inside the allowance and take limit, no further gates. Ben sees the takes and clicks accept or reject on the board. With Q2, that click mints the `take` receipt; there is no second confirmation of the same choice. Without Q2, the decision is `usage_status` in the asset manifest plus the flag, and the plan says so.

Recorded automatically, never asked: every prompt, reference list with hashes, provider job id, cost, output hash (generation receipts, existing); every reservation and settlement (cost ledger, existing); every rejected take, kept as history (asset manifest, existing).

New authorization is needed when: the allowance or take limit would be exceeded; the beat, identity, setting or staging changes materially; a new provider or a new data-sharing class is needed (that is a config signature, existing, and this plan does not touch config).

Three decisions is the normal successful path, not a ceiling. Attention can still be required by: a revised storyboard (new batch signature); a requested increase of allowance or take limit (new D-A); an indeterminate paid job, which `paid_call_context` already blocks until reconciled (`tools/video/_shared.py:1033-1035`, `tools/cost_tracker.py:731-750`). That block is financial protection and stays.

## Changes (minimum set)

- **C1 Manifest `authored-film@1.6`.** 1.5 byte-for-byte plus `partial_predecessors: [look_lock, headshots, visual_bible]` on the `script`, `scene_plan` and `assets` stages. `lib/checkpoint.py` reads the key from the manifest stage spec, else the code default. Allowed values: only stages that precede the declaring stage in the same manifest, and only the statuses in `PARTIAL_STATUSES` (`in_progress`, `awaiting_human`); a missing, failed or malformed predecessor never satisfies. Load-time validation rejects unknown stages, later stages, self-reference and non-list values. `edit` and `compose` keep requiring a completed bible (R2). Bible completion semantics unchanged (R3). 1.6 registered in `LOOK_LOCK_MANIFESTS`, `QC_MANIFESTS`, `HERO_QC_MANIFESTS`, `HERO_BATCH_MANIFESTS`; `require_migration_coverage` 1.6 branch refuses migration while a governed request is pending or a run state is non-empty; 1.5 manifest digest frozen by golden test. D21 renumbers to 1.7 when resumed.
- **C2 Shot allowance (dollars) and video take limit (count), enforced through the reservation lifecycle.**
  - Fields: the scene_plan shot record gains `spend_allowance_usd` (one shared dollar allowance for every paid call named for that shot: storyboard frames, video takes, any ancillary call) and `max_video_takes` (video generation attempts only; storyboard calls consume dollars, never a take). Additive optional fields; the implementer states whether the scene_plan schema admits them at 1.1 or needs a minor bump, and why.
  - Authority: the allowance is read from the shot's record in the on-disk, pin-bound scene_plan checkpoint (the read the boundary already performs at `_scene_plan_shot_index`), never from caller inputs. On the shot path (a call carrying `asset_class: shot_visual`, or a storyboard-frame call naming `shot_id`), a missing shot id, an unresolvable shot record, or missing allowance fields REFUSE; there is no fallback to project-only accounting. Non-shot governed calls keep today's project-cap-only behavior, tested separately.
  - Ordering, verified: both paid tools upload references before `reserve_paid_call` (`tools/video/kling_reference_video.py:325-342`, `tools/graphics/seedream_image.py:253-272`). So the check runs twice: first in `paid_call_context` (before any upload), computing the scope's spent plus outstanding reservations plus attempt count and refusing; then again inside `reserve_paid_call`, project and scope balances together under the existing reservation lock, so two concurrent calls cannot both pass the first check. Reservation events persist `scope: shot:<shot_id>` and `kind: video|image`, carried through attach, settlement, failure and reconciliation, so cumulative spend and attempt count survive restart and are rebuilt from the ledger, not from memory.
  - Counting rules: a video attempt is consumed when a reservation for a NEW video request is created. Release of dollars and attempt happens only when NON-ACCEPTANCE IS CONFIRMED (the provider returned a definite rejection or the submission demonstrably never left the machine). A missing provider request id is not proof of non-acceptance: a timeout after acceptance but before the id was saved leaves the reservation in `submitting`, which stays outstanding, keeps its attempt, and routes to reconciliation exactly as today (`tools/cost_tracker.py:584-600`, `resume_check`); reconciliation settles it `completed` or `failed` from the provider's answer, and only a confirmed `failed` releases. Once a provider request id is attached, dollars and the attempt are consumed whatever the outcome. (Codex, revision-2 review: a missing id does not prove the provider never accepted.) Polling, resuming or reconciling an existing job never creates a reservation and never consumes anything. A rejected take refunds nothing and keeps its attempt. Changing the approved package does not reset money or attempts already spent on that shot id; a larger allowance or take limit is a new D-A decision, recorded by the same flags.
- **C3 Pre-upload ownership check, narrow.** Applies only to governed calls under a 1.6 pin that pass a `reference_manifest`; older pins and calls without a manifest are untouched, with tests on both boundaries. In `bind_reference_manifest`, each reference asset must be owned by an approved bible entry for its `visual_bible_entity_id` (`approved_image_owners` of the on-disk visual_bible checkpoint), refusing before upload or reservation. The only storyboard exemption is the tool-packed, re-hashed frame covered by the shot's signed batch receipt (`storyboard_preflight`); caller-supplied entries labelled "storyboard" stay rejected exactly as today (`_shared.py:1258-1261`).
- **C4 (Q2) `take` receipt kind.** `APPROVAL_KINDS += "take"`; record `{project_id, shot_id, take asset_id, generation_receipt_id, usage_status ∈ selected|rejected}`; constructor in `gate_approve` reads the pending assets checkpoint and the generation receipt, digest-bound like a sheet gate; the board renders the take with its frame and references. Ben's accept or reject on the board is the one action: it mints the receipt and sets `usage_status`. There is no conversational approval that is then repeated in a signing step. Enforcement: `_check_assets_v11` requires a matching verified `take` receipt for every `selected` take under a 1.6 pin; rejected takes stay in the manifest as history. With C4 the per-shot signature count is two (storyboard, take).

Compatibility claim, stated exactly: existing records remain valid and verify unchanged. C2 adds optional fields to shot records and reservation events; C4 adds a new receipt kind. No existing receipt, record shape, director or config is edited, and no programmatic approval path is added.

## Effort and risk (bounded expectations, not facts)

- C1 + C3 + tests T1–T9: one build task, comparable to one D21 step. C2 + T10: a second task, now the largest of the three (two fields, scoped reservation events through settlement and reconciliation, two-point check with the lock, ledger rebuild). Risk to existing approvals: expected none, established only when T5 and T6 pass. D21 compatibility: expected renumbering only; confirmed when D21 resumes against 1.6.
- Per-shot cost for the experiment: one storyboard frame about $0.07, one ten-second take about $1.12 without audio; an allowance of a few dollars covers a small number of retries. No amount is authorized by this plan.
