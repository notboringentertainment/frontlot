# The trailer paper cut

The film list has a start panel. It keeps the title Ben types and gives the folder a unique name so punctuation is safe. Creation is staged privately until both the production and its signing request exist. Overlapping requests for the same name make one trailer; later attempts to reuse the name are refused. An interrupted older draft can make its missing signing request again. Signing creation approves no stage and permits no paid work. Spending still needs the app’s separate signed spending settings.

Ben can save beats, spoken lines, cards and a timed shot list as a draft. Until approved story material is available, the board says the draft has not yet been checked and later stages cannot use it. Story approval happens outside these panels for now; its control is not built. When that approval arrives, the saved draft is checked immediately on the next board update, and failures are shown for Ben to fix.

A trailer may leave protected lines out. Each line or card resembling protected dialogue must reproduce it exactly. The existing story checker compares normalized wording with a conservative threshold of seventy-five percent character similarity for phrases of at least three words. Exact normalized matches and embedded protected wording are recognized too. Once recognized, capitalization and punctuation must match the original. This is a local wording check, not a claim to understand every paraphrase. Spoiler references, exact reveals and close paraphrases are refused in viewer text. Beats and shot purposes are private planning notes and show warnings instead. Ben still reviews the complete trailer for meaning and spoilers.

The temporary sequence contains labelled placeholders, with no footage or sound. It cannot finish a stage or create a generated asset. Private shot purposes are not presented as trailer pictures. Finished-media editing, story signing and export remain later work.

Every completed save preserves the version it replaces. Saved versions appear in order with their times, update without a page reload, and open with the wording, timing, references and difficulty intact. Restoring makes another version and keeps every earlier one. A stale window can open the latest save while keeping its unsaved draft. Saving identical content again does not manufacture another version.

There is one authoritative edit record. If it already contains a rough cut, changing the paper cut keeps that cut’s timing and footage and marks the downstream work as needing a fresh look. The paper cut is a proposed revision, never a silent replacement of the later cut. Creative input changes require review; changing a checkpoint’s approval status or timestamp alone does not create an endless review cycle.

## For the tools

The authoritative file remains `artifacts/edit_decisions.json`. Integer frame timing is at 24 fps, with half-open picture intervals. Planned speech is in `paper_cut.lines`; title timing is in `timeline.cards`; placeholder picture timing is in `timeline.picture`. Older productions retain their own contracts. No asset manifest, receipt or completed checkpoint is written by the editor.

`lib.trailer_edit.save_revision` requires `expected_revision` (including `None` for the first save). `save_edit_revision` is the shared persistence boundary for later cutting code; supplying an expected revision adds compare-and-swap protection. Generic app writes through `lib.state_io.atomic_write_bytes` or `atomic_write_json` route trailer edit records through that boundary and preserve whatever they replace. Cutting code should call `save_edit_revision` with its caller’s revision rather than writing the path directly.

A single atomic replacement commits the current edit plus a flat `revision_history` of previous committed snapshots. History and its legacy file-time lookup are excluded from `edit_digest`; every snapshot has its own verified digest. `saved_at`, `revision_number` and `previous_revision` identify order and time. For older edits without a recorded save time, `revision_times` preserves their file modification times before replacement; the UI labels these as file times. Old archive records are read only if reachable through the committed previous-version chain; disconnected failed saves are never listed. Temporary files are cleaned on failure and abandoned writer files are cleaned under the edit lock at the next read or save. A failed pre-commit write leaves the previous record and history unchanged.

`paper_cut_pending` preserves a later rough cut while identifying the paper changes that still need reconciliation. `paper_state.usable_downstream` requires an approved story check and fresh creative inputs. Approval records for later stages refuse unchecked, failed or stale drafts. The fingerprint covers explicit story, treatment and script paths, including absent files, and uses their artifacts rather than approval bookkeeping.

The protected-line matcher lives in `lib.canon_enforcement`, alongside existing story enforcement. Spoiler paraphrase matching normalizes death and closing word forms and requires the revealing description after the fact’s linking verb as well as at least two meaningful shared words, two thirds of the candidate’s meaningful vocabulary and half the protected fact’s vocabulary. This catches the reviewed caretaker reveal without rejecting the honest museum-closed card. It cannot recognize every semantic paraphrase.

Capability and origin protected writes use `POST /api/trailers`, `POST /api/project/{project_id}/trailer-creation-request`, `POST /api/project/{project_id}/paper-cut`, and `POST /api/project/{project_id}/paper-cut/restore`. Read-only status and version access use `GET /api/project/{project_id}/paper-cut` and its revision suffix. The embedded assistant’s `trailer_paper_save` requires the revision used to prepare its draft and snapshots explicit absent upstream files. It cannot create or approve trailers.
