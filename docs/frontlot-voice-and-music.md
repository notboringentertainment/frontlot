# Voice and music operations

This is the service and take-record half of voice and music. It adds no panels, screens or buttons. The voice catalogue, pronunciation and delivery fields, spending card, player, native file chooser and paper-cut panels belong to the second half.

An operation prepares a request from an exact saved spoken line or an instrumental music brief. Speech keeps the line's wording and whole-frame timing. Music keeps its exact brief and requested whole-frame duration. The existing signing gate reconstructs that request and checks it again before Ben signs. A caller cannot override the words, voice, model, duration or price when submitting it.

Voice requires the paper cut's current “usable by later stages” result, not an editable saved check flag. This is checked when preparing, signing and sending, and when using an accepted take. Unchecked story material, changed inputs and a pending paper-cut revision refuse audio. Trailer music briefs require the same checked, current paper cut: this half has no separate declaration that a trailer brief is independent of its story. Older independent briefs without a paper cut keep their existing protected route.

Prices come from Ben's signed production settings. Each rate has its own date and expiry. The settings name the dollar replacement value of a credit, the highest speech credits per character, the highest music credits per minute and, for voice selection, a multiplier ceiling. There are no default rates. Until the catalogue can establish which voices have standard rates, every speech request requires a signed multiplier ceiling. Missing, future-dated or expired rates refuse approval. Changing the settings requires a new request and approval.

The operation reserves the computed maximum before making one service submission. It neither retries nor follows redirects. Successful audio with usable billing evidence is saved through the existing recovery record, signed generation receipt and production spending ledger. The credit charge is valued using the signed rate and counted once; it is not also entered as a new cash purchase. No top-up or plan change is performed. Missing billing evidence, excess charges, interrupted downloads and uncertain outcomes retain the reservation for account review.

A generated take records its approved request and generation receipt. An imported music take records its source, licence note and file fingerprint. Imports require a plain, supported audio file without linked folders, linked files or path tricks. The caller must already have obtained the path from the native chooser; this half does not open that chooser. Files are copied into the production, probed locally and kept under new names. A licence note records Ben's evidence; it does not establish legal clearance.

An import is refused if its bytes match any generation receipt in this production. Editing a generated take to claim that it was imported is also refused: the signed generation and spending receipts retain its origin and kind. This check does not recognise a generated file that was re-encoded or altered elsewhere, or generated in another production whose receipts are not here. Different bytes have a different fingerprint. In that case the licence note remains Ben's own statement about where the music came from; the software does not certify it.

Every generated music take and its signing evidence say: “For the test trailer only until a person at ElevenLabs confirms otherwise in writing.” Music submission always requests instrumental output. A caller cannot opt into vocals.

A take starts as “Waiting for your decision.” Ben can sign acceptance or decline through the existing gate. A signed decline is final for that take, including after changes to its line or brief. It always reads “Declined” and cannot be accepted or used again. Changing his mind means a new take, with its own request and spending approval. An acceptance can be signed once and can later be retired by a decline. A changed file, line, timing or music brief makes an accepted take read “Needs a fresh look.” Every signed decision must belong to that exact take; a signature filed under another take does not apply. The operation that supplies audio for a cut refuses anything other than an accepted, unchanged take with verified provenance. Later cutting and export code must call that operation again when consuming audio, rather than trusting cached labels.

Account review is a separate signed operation. Ben records what he checked, whether a request was charged, and its actual cash and replacement-credit amounts. Only the exact signed outcome can settle that reservation. Once an outcome is signed, another cannot replace it. Conflicting signed outcomes leave spending blocked for investigation. A live service call cannot be resolved while it still owns its work. The original spending approval stays used even when the account outcome is “not charged.” Time passing never resolves an unknown charge. If no audio was recovered, settling its bill does not invent a take.

All older ElevenLabs voice and music calls now need approval of each request too. Valid older settings no longer open an unapproved route. They still load under their unchanged schemas, but audio needs new signed price settings and an individual signed request. There is no automatic migration. Voice selection never falls back to another provider or account. Explicit older selections of a different provider remain explicit selections, and a known unresolved audio reservation blocks their network-audio calls too.

A signature has the same limits as Frontlot's other local signatures. It proves integrity under the local signing key and the recorded approval path. It does not prove safety against another program running as the owner with access to that key.

For a trailer, other voice, music-generation and outside audio tools refuse before service contact. They also refuse when the production is unidentified, unregistered, linked, copied outside its registered place or cannot verify its signed settings and history. The remaining legacy route requires a positively verified registered older production with the older settings. Local mixing, enhancement, library lookup and probing stay local and make no outside call. Optional event reporting cannot disable this refusal. The protected ElevenLabs route and voice selector continue to require an individual signed request for every production.

## For the tools

The additive settings branch is v1_4, selected by version 1.4 in schemas/project_config.schema.json. Versions 1.0 through 1.3 retain their definitions. audio_price_basis has dollars_per_credit, speech_credits_per_character and music_credits_per_minute, plus voice_multiplier_ceiling for speech. Each rate contains value, set_at and expires_at. Dates are Unix seconds. Quotes round credit units up, then dollar bounds up to four decimal places. Speech counts UTF-8 bytes conservatively so non-ASCII text cannot undercount characters.

lib.audio_prices.quote reads the latest verified signed settings. An unsigned draft can only explain a missing-price-basis refusal; it cannot authorize spending. lib.audio_workspace exposes save_music_brief, prepare_request, request_decision, require_accepted, request_resolution, resolve_charge and state. The paper-cut reader consumes paper_cut.lines from artifacts/edit_decisions.json and checks edit_revision_sha256 using the existing edit_digest. The paper-cut piece is now merged. Integration tests save lines through lib.trailer_edit.save_revision and bind the complete row, including text, start_frame, duration_frames and source_ref. Changes to any of these make an accepted take need a fresh look. Music briefs and their previous versions live below .audio-workspace/briefs. The second half must connect the panels and apply the paper-cut story and freshness checks when exposing the operations.

Generated requests extend the existing audio request additively with binding and price_basis_sha256. Older request receipts remain readable; the service adapter and current signer refuse requests without the current signed price basis and source binding. The approval kinds audio_take and audio_resolution reuse the existing signing and verification machinery; audio_spend remains the paid-request kind. No stage is completed by any of these operations.

The wrapper inputs are project_dir and audio_request_id, with an optional output_path that must resolve to the take's governed location. The leading tilde is expanded before that comparison. Legacy generation properties are deprecated, and supplying them alongside a saved request is refused. _generate delegates to the same protected entry point and never uses its caller-supplied credential. The only credential source is the server's ELEVENLABS_API_KEY.

The HTTP adapter uses one requests.post to the fixed ElevenLabs speech or music endpoint with allow_redirects=False. It requires a successful status, a request-id and character-cost billing metadata. Unavailable or invalid metadata leaves billing pending. character-cost is converted at the signed dollars_per_credit; no guessed charge is substituted. The credit-funded adapter records zero additional cash and the replacement-credit amount. An account or response using a different billing convention must be reviewed rather than guessed. No account service was contacted to test live header behavior.

The first half creates no new HTTP operation endpoint. App adapters call these narrow Python operations and use the existing gate requests. The second half must preserve capability and origin checks when exposing them, obtain imported paths through the native chooser, and keep credentials out of page data. It must add no retry or provider substitution around the operations.

Known limits are the absence of live service verification, live account or voice eligibility checks, and automated re-download after account resolution. A resolved charge with no completed generation receipt remains a paid attempt without a usable take. Successful receipt recovery recreates a missing take without another submission. Existing whole-stage approval restrictions remain in force.
