# Voice and music workbench

The trailer board has voice and music workspaces beside its paper cut. It shows saved lines beside protected wording, reads account voices on request, and prepares one spending card at a time. No voice is selected by default. The writer confirms permission to use the selected voice.

The card shows the actual saved words, ElevenLabs, the fixed Multilingual v2 model, the selected account voice, the intended line slot, maximum cash and replacement-credit charges, expiry and remaining production ceiling. The existing signing buttons approve that exact request. A separate button then submits it through the existing protected tool. Reloading or pressing again cannot buy a second call with that approval.

The speech endpoint does not receive pronunciation or delivery notes from this model adapter. Notes stay with the signed request for human review, and the panel explicitly says so. The endpoint determines actual audio length. Listen before keeping a take; neither the paper-cut slot nor a note is a promise of performance or timing.

The writer hears a take and signs keeping or permanent decline. The current line changing makes a kept take need fresh review. Declining a stale take remains possible, because refusing to use it does not approve changed source material. Decline remains final. Another take starts another spending card and requires another signature.

Audio prices and account consent can be prepared and signed without a terminal. Rates have no defaults. The form records speech and music ceilings, the credit replacement value, a voice multiplier of at least one, and a future expiry. It adds consent for separately approved speech and instrumental music briefs. Existing production permissions remain part of the full configuration signature. Saving a new draft pauses paid work until signing; the latest signed settings can be explicitly restored from a preserved copy. Audio-only settings do not require a picture judge; picture judging still requires its own complete settings and consent.

The price review leads with what the signature permits, the dollar bound for the longest saved spoken line and for one minute of music, the whole trailer ceiling and the earliest expiry. The rate details and other production settings are below “Details of these settings”. This changes the presentation, not the signed record. A different line or cue still needs its own spending card and signature.

For music, save an exact brief and a length in whole frames, then prepare and sign one instrumental request. Generated music says “For the test trailer only until a person at ElevenLabs confirms otherwise in writing” on the card, the take and the signing review. Listen, then sign keeping or declining it. Changing the saved brief or its timing makes a kept take need a fresh look. Decline remains final; a new attempt needs a new request and signature.

To bring in music, press “Choose a music file”. The Mac running Front Lot opens its native file chooser. Write where the music came from and its licence note, then bring it in at no charge. The take can be played and its exact file, source and licence note signed. Linked files, linked folders and path tricks are refused. A file matching generated audio in this production is refused as an import. Re-encoded or changed files, music generated in another production and music made outside Front Lot may have no matching receipt here; the licence note remains the writer's statement, not independent legal clearance.

An uncertain bill stays held against the ceiling. The board exposes the existing signed account-review operation and applies only its signed outcome. It makes no new submission and creates no missing audio. Cash and replacement-credit audio charges are separated, while the production total includes all services.

The voice and music panels share their request cards, take states and production total. Replacement credits and new cash are shown separately, counted once against the same ceiling. A held audio charge blocks new paid submissions of either kind. Imports make no paid call. The panels add no direction-text synthesis, voice cloning, automatic retries, provider substitution, whole-stage approval, asset-manifest completion or cutting/export control. The later cut must call the existing accepted-take operation again when consuming audio. No older-production compatibility layer was added.

## For the tools

The panel adapter is backlot/voice_panel.py and its view is backlot/ui/voice.js. Every write route uses the board origin and capability check. Paid submission calls ElevenLabsTTS with only project_dir and audio_request_id. Account-list retrieval uses the fixed ElevenLabs voices endpoint, with redirects and retries off, and sends no story. Credentials come only from the server environment and never appear in the page or saved request.

The existing audio_spend, audio_take, audio_resolution and config gate kinds remain the only signature authorities. Price requests use the audio:project_config scope and a digest checked again by the configuration constructor. The existing configuration schema's current audio branch permits omitted qc; a supplied qc block still validates fully. No judge or price is invented. The broker and signer use the running Python environment so the app's own libraries are present.

New notes and the account voice label are kept inside the signed source binding. The paid tool's outgoing speech body remains exactly text and model_id, with voice selected by its approved URL. Content playback is confined to one verified take, refuses links and mismatched bytes, and serves the locally probed audio type. Music uses MusicGen through the same protected route with only project_dir and audio_request_id; the fixed panel model is music_v1. Its provider payload binds prompt, model_id, music_length_ms and force_instrumental=true. Live model availability and billing conventions have not been verified by a service call.

backlot/music_panel.py supplies the narrowly named music routes and the native chooser adapter. backlot/ui/music.js supplies the brief and import forms inside the shared audio view. The server holds chosen paths in memory for fifteen minutes, returns only a selection token and filename to the page, and refuses tokens belonging to another production. The chooser uses a fixed osascript program without a shell or interpolated user text. Cancelling preserves the previous selection and says so. Restarting the board requires choosing the file again. Imports reuse lib.audio_workspace.import_music and existing audio_take signatures.
